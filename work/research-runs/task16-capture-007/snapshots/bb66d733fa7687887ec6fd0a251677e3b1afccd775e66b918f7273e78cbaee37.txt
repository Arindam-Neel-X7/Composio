A short, video-first guide to Consensus for researchers at any level. Learn to search, read, and organise the literature on your topic, one lesson at a time.
Copy pageCopy page
Consensus is an AI search engine for research. You ask a question, it searches over 400 million scholarly articles, including 350 million peer-reviewed research papers, and it writes an answer where every sentence links back to the paper it came from.
It’s a search engine first. It finds the papers, then uses AI to read and summarize them. It doesn’t make things up from general knowledge, so you can always click through and check the source.
How this guide works
This guide is for researchers at any level, whether you’re starting your first project or you’ve been publishing for years. Each lesson is a short video plus a prompt to try on your own topic. Start at the top if you’re new to Consensus, or jump straight to the task you need.
1. Quick start
Run a search, see where the papers come from, and save what you find.
2. Understand your results
Read an answer, check its claims, and find the strongest papers.
3. Map the literature
Find the main findings, the history, and where studies disagree.
4. Follow the trail
Use citations to go from one good paper to many.
5. Organise and find gaps
Keep your papers in one place and spot what’s missing.
Key features
Reference pages for search, Research Agent, Deep Review, Library, and more.
All the videos are also in one YouTube playlist.

Why researchers trust Consensus
Search happens before AI. The AI only summarizes papers Consensus actually found. If nothing relevant turns up, it tells you.
Every claim is checkable. Hover a citation to see the exact quote from the paper. See Check a claim against the paper.
Retracted papers are flagged and never used in answers.
Your data isn’t training data. Consensus doesn’t train models on your searches or uploads.
It’s measured. In blinded evaluations judged by PhD researchers, Consensus finds more relevant papers than Google Scholar and than frontier models with web search. See Benchmarks.
More relevant than Google Scholar
88.1% vs 81.8% precision on 500 real user queries, judged blind by PhD researchers.
Beats GPT-5.5 and Opus 4.8, 10x faster
More relevant top-20 papers at a median of 8.7 seconds, versus 75 to 103 seconds.
AI is a research assistant, not a replacement for reading. Use it to find and understand papers faster, then read the ones that matter.
